# Growth memo, Q3

We sell payroll and time off software to HR leads at companies of 50 to 500 people. Most of them still run
payroll on spreadsheets with an outside gestor, and that is the alternative we lose to. What they buy from us is
closing payroll in one afternoon; our local payroll rules engine is what makes that possible, and it in turn
depends on our payments bank to move salaries. Search ads on payroll queries bring most trials. Customers pay a
per employee subscription, billed monthly.

We ended Q2 with 1,240 paying accounts (board deck p.4). New accounts per month add to that and cancelled
accounts per month drain it. Happy admins invite peers, so more paying accounts means more referral invites, and
those turn into new accounts about two months later. But every new account brings setup questions, which pile up
as open support tickets, and slow answers are the top reason accounts cancel. Onboarding specialists cut the
ticket load; their headcount is set against our goal of monthly churn under 1.5%.
